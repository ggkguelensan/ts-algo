Lazy selections avoid an intermediate refs array and let limit stop traversal. Large full-window pages therefore do different amounts of work from eager selections while returning the same DTOs. Prepared native loops still win over the public abstraction. Full materialization pays generator/Source cost: small selective results can be relatively slower than the prototype, and large long/nested results can lose to native scans or legacy arrays. The fixed microsecond-scale overhead is interpreted with the predeclared absolute-cost criterion; it is not concealed as a speedup. The contract trades this overhead for repeatability, live binding and early stop.
